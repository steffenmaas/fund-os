# Fund OS Writing Style Guide

Research-backed rules for all public-facing content (LinkedIn posts, blog articles, op-eds, newsletters, sector reports). Apply to every draft. These rules outrank generic writing habits.

Sources: readability research (~140 years: short sentences boost comprehension; 15–25 word ceiling), LinkedIn algorithm studies 2026 (dwell time, expertise-based distribution, first-2-lines cutoff), Sinek's Golden Circle, story-arc frameworks (Fichtean curve, hero's journey for brands).

## 1. Structure: Why → What → How

- Open with the WHY: the belief, the stakes, or the pain. Never with the product.
- Then WHAT: the concept or insight, concrete and specific.
- Then HOW: how it works in practice, with real numbers.
- Close with what changes for the reader, then the outlook.

## 2. The hook (first 2 lines)

- The first two lines decide everything. LinkedIn truncates after ~2–3 lines.
- Use one of: a counterintuitive claim, a painful shared experience, or a confession.
- Good: "A small fund dies a quiet death, and it never shows up in the portfolio. It shows up in the calendar."
- Bad: "In today's fast-paced world of venture capital..."

## 3. Sentences and paragraphs

- One idea per sentence. Max ~15–25 words. Cut every sentence that can be cut.
- Paragraphs: 1–3 sentences. White space is a feature.
- Verbs over nouns. Active over passive. Concrete over abstract.
- If a word can be removed without losing meaning, remove it.

## 4. Story arc

- Tension early: introduce the conflict or failure in the first third.
- Strip options away one by one (failed attempts work well) before the resolution.
- The reader is the hero. The fund is the guide. Show transformation with proof.
- Real numbers beat adjectives: "six hours to thirty minutes" beats "dramatically faster".

## 5. Voice

- Founder voice: first person, direct, honest about failures. Build-in-public beats polish.
- Confident and specific. No superlatives, no "thought leadership" language.
- Write from actual experience. Generic advice gets pushed down by the algorithm and by readers.
- Personal motivation is allowed and effective when authentic.

## 6. Anti-AI-artefact checklist (apply before every output)

Remove or rewrite if present:
- Em-dash chains and triadic flourishes ("X, Y, and Z" repeated as a rhetorical tic)
- Formulaic parallelism ("It's not about X. It's about Y.") more than once per piece
- Stock phrases: "in today's landscape", "game-changer", "unlock", "delve", "embark", "elevate", "seamless", "robust", "leverage" (as verb), "at the end of the day"
- Empty intensifiers: "truly", "genuinely", "incredibly", "really"
- Bold-label list formatting where flowing prose reads better
- Headers that announce instead of intrigue ("Introduction", "Conclusion")
- Perfectly uniform sentence rhythm — vary length deliberately
- Hedge stacking ("can potentially help to...")

## 7. Headlines (data-backed rules)

Sources: BuzzSumo (10M LinkedIn articles, 100M headlines), Backlinko (912M posts), meta-analysis of 8,977 headline experiments.

- **Lead with "How":** "How to / How a / How we" headlines earn ~3x more shares than any other opener in B2B LinkedIn content.
- **Nine words.** Optimal LinkedIn headline length. Stay within 7–11.
- **Concreteness sweet spot:** specific enough to be credible, vague enough to leave a curiosity gap. Never disclose the answer in the headline. Avoid abstract jargon endings ("...implements agentic operations" → "...runs on AI").
- **Top LinkedIn phrases:** "the future of", "X ways to", "need to know".
- **Questions beat declaratives** on click-through. Use sparingly, reader-directed.
- **Numbers:** 5 and 10 perform best in B2B; short lists beat long ones.
- **Always generate 5+ headline options** across patterns (how, question, future-of, number-led) and recommend one with rationale.

Examples:
- Good: "How Two People Run an Entire VC Fund" (8 words, how-opener, concrete, gap)
- Bad: "Fund OS: A Comprehensive Agentic Operations Framework for Emerging Managers" (jargon, no gap, too long)

## 8. Format rules by mode

- **short (LinkedIn post):** max 200 words. Hook in line 1–2. Line breaks between thoughts. One CTA max, soft.
- **long (blog/op-ed):** 600–1,200 words, ~4 min read target. Subheads every 150–250 words. One image or visual anchor.
- **report:** executive summary first, findings in descending order of importance, sources at the end.

## 9. Quality gate

Before filing a draft, verify:
1. Would the first two lines stop a scroll?
2. Is the WHY stated before the product?
3. Is every claim backed by a number or a named source?
4. Did the anti-artefact checklist pass?
5. Reading time at or under target?
6. Headline: how/question/future-of pattern, 7–11 words, curiosity gap intact?

If any check fails, revise before filing. Then file under /Drafts/Public-Content/ as usual — partner review remains mandatory.
